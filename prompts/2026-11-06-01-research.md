# 2026-11-06 · Session 01 · From research to a working prototype

**Tool:** Claude (desktop app, working in this repo)
**Phases:** 1 research, 2 define, 3 design, 4 build

Each entry keeps the prompt **as I wrote it** (the honest record) and adds a **refined prompt**: what I would write now, with the context, constraints and checks spelled out. Comparing the two shows how my prompting got sharper.

---

### Prompt 1 · Research
**As written:** "OK, now let's get back to working on CRM Rethought."
**Refined prompt:**
> You're my research partner on CRM Rethought: a public, clean room redesign of a B2B CRM for account teams at SaaS companies (decisions 0001 to 0003). Use only the linked findings in `research/desk-research.md`. Write three things:
> 1. A 30 minute interview guide with open questions, no leading ones.
> 2. Five SIMULATED interviews, one per persona in `research/personas.md`. Every answer cites the finding it rests on.
> 3. A synthesis: count each pain across the five interviews, write jobs to be done, and propose the flows worth building in depth, each with a measurable target.
>
> Label every simulated artifact SIMULATED. Flag any pain that rests on a single source.

**Why:** Phase 0 was done (repo public, site live); time to start research.
**What came back:** an interview guide, five SIMULATED interviews (one per persona, every answer tied to a desk research finding), a synthesis that counts seven pains across the five, five jobs to be done, and four flows to build in depth with targets to measure them by.
**My call:** Kept the four flows (My day, Meeting brief, Log a call, Deals at risk) and the idea that source links and one move record fixes run through all of them. Made Imports a light screen.

### Prompt 2 · Converge
**As written:** "Next is converge. Which one of the ideas that came out of UX research will we be picking, and why?"
**Refined prompt:**
> Score the four flows from `research/synthesis.md` on four criteria: how many pains each answers, how often people would use it, whether the win can be measured, and how different it is from today's CRMs. Show the scores as a table and a 2x2 of value against difference. Recommend one thing to build first, say what waits, and name the honest risk: which competitors already ship part of it. Record the call as a decision.

**Why:** research produced four flows; the design phase needs one focus.
**What came back:** the meeting loop (Meeting brief before a call, Log a call after it) scored highest on value and difference, with a 2x2 and a criteria table; My day as a light entry point; Deals at risk later. It also named the risk: HubSpot and Attio ship parts of this already.
**My call:** Took the meeting loop (brief before the call, log after it) forward as the first build, and asked for the reasoning to go into the case study.

### Prompt 3 · Choices and competitors
**As written:** "Add this as a section to the case study, justifying the choices we made. Do a competitive analysis of similar existing products in the market and save it as another section of our case study."
**Refined prompt:**
> Two new case study sections, written for an interviewer who asks "why this?" and "doesn't HubSpot already do that?".
> 1. **Choices we made:** every decision so far, the options weighed and the reason, each linked to its record in `decisions/`.
> 2. **Competitive analysis:** compare Salesforce Agentforce, HubSpot Breeze, Attio, Gong, Clari, Day.ai and Granola on six things: a brief before a meeting, call logs into the CRM, deal risk with a reason, a source on every AI line, approval before saving, and what changed since last time. Open each vendor's page and cite it. Mark anything from a third party, and say "not mentioned" rather than "no".

**Why:** an interviewer will ask "why this?" and "doesn't HubSpot already do that?"; both answers should be on the page.
**What came back:** a table of the eight choices so far, each linked to its decision record, and a matrix of seven products (Salesforce Agentforce Sales, HubSpot Breeze, Attio, Gong, Clari, Day.ai, Granola) against six capabilities, read from public pages. Finding: briefs and call logs are common; a source on every AI line is not offered by any of them, and approval is a setting rather than the design.
**My call:** Asked for a wider check before trusting the gap (Prompt 4).

### Prompt 4 · Is it already built?
**As written:** "Check if an app already exists that's almost similar to the idea we are about to build." Then: "Yes, but add an interesting visual or data viz to demo this comparative study, one slide on the case study."
**Refined prompt:**
> Before we design anything, try to prove the idea already exists. Search for AI sales tools that lead a brief with what changed, draft the log from the call, link every line to its source, and wait for a person to approve. Read each product's own pages. Tell me which come closest, and what is still open. Then turn all 13 products into one visual that fits on a single case study slide: the finding should be readable in five seconds.

**Why:** before designing, make sure the idea isn't a copy, and know what to say when an interviewer names a competitor.
**What came back:** six more products (Sybill, Sentra, Lightfield, AskElephant, Rox, Momentum). None does the whole loop; Sybill and Sentra already lead briefs with what changed, so the difference narrowed to the trust layer. A coverage grid of 13 products by 6 capabilities replaced the table in the case study.
**My call:** Accepted that the difference is the trust layer, not "what changed" alone, and picked the coverage grid as the slide.

### Prompt 5 · Next phase
**As written:** "Done, let's move on to the next phase." Answers to setup questions: wireframes coded and live; show 2 to 3 visual directions before tokens; this session covers the information architecture and wireframes.
**Refined prompt:**
> Close Phase 2 and start Phase 3.
> - **Phase 2:** map each step of the meeting loop to what the AI does and what a person decides, and write the rule as a decision.
> - **Phase 3:** an information architecture (objects, navigation, screen map), then grayscale wireframes of Today, Meeting brief and Log a call. Build them in code so the approve in place pattern can be clicked, not just looked at.
>
> Keep WCAG AA in light and dark, check with axe, and log decisions as you go.

**Why:** research and converge are settled; the meeting loop needs a structure people can click.
**What came back:** Phase 2 closed with a "where AI belongs" map and decision 0008 (AI drafts; people decide). Phase 3 started: an information architecture built on one new object, the suggestion, a screen map, and three grayscale wireframes in code (Today, Meeting brief, Log a call) with a source drawer and approve in place (decision 0009).
**My call:** Chose coded, live wireframes over drawings; asked to see 2 to 3 visual directions before any tokens; kept this session to the information architecture and wireframes.

### Prompt 6 · Visual directions
**As written:** "OK that worked, let's get to building the next phase."
**Refined prompt:**
> Show me three visual directions on the same piece of a meeting brief, in light and dark. Each needs a name, a mood, a type pairing and a palette, with contrast ratios computed on the page. Each one must also answer the question the product rests on: at a glance, how do you tell a line the AI drafted from a fact a person approved?

**Why:** the wireframes passed the browser check; tokens need a direction first, and I asked to see 2 to 3 options.
**What came back:** a /directions page with three looks applied to the same brief fragment: Ledger (calm, editorial, deep green), Signal (dense, technical, electric blue) and Margin note (warm, clay, sticky note drafts). Each proposes a different way to tell an AI draft from an approved fact, and prints its own contrast ratios in light and dark.
**My call:** Asked which direction fits the tool and the jobs I'm applying for (Prompt 7) before choosing.

### Prompt 7 · Pick a direction
**As written:** "Which one of A, B, C suits better for the tool we are building and the jobs we are applying for?" Then: "Yes" to Signal with a 14px base and the mono source chip as the signature.
**Refined prompt:**
> Which of the three directions best fits (a) a tool account teams keep open all day and (b) the companies I'm applying to: data dense SaaS, fintech and enterprise tools? Argue it on density, how a draft is marked for accessibility, and how hiring managers there will read it, not on taste. Then turn the winner into tokens: color with computed contrast, type scale, space, shape and the signature components.

**Why:** the choice should serve the users and the audience for the case study, not taste alone.
**What came back:** Signal, for density, for mono sources that read as system facts, for a DRAFT tag that is a word rather than a style, and for fit with data dense SaaS and fintech products. Then the token set: colors with computed contrast, type scale, space, shape and the signature components, live at /tokens (decision 0010).
**My call:** Picked Signal, with a 14px base instead of 13px and the mono source chip as the signature element.

### Prompt 8 · Build it
**As written:** "Wait, let's first build the entire working prototype locally." Scope answer: the full meeting loop.
**Refined prompt:**
> Build a working prototype of the full meeting loop in Signal, at `/prototype`: Today, Meeting brief, Log a call, Accounts, an account record and Review. State has to carry across screens. Approving a log line puts it on the account timeline, and the next meeting's brief is built only from approved lines. Use sample data only. Check every page with axe in light and dark, at desktop and phone width, before calling it done.

**Why:** the case study needs something people can use, not only screens to look at.
**What came back:** /prototype in Signal: Today, Meeting brief, Log a call, Accounts, an account record with an approved timeline, and Review. Approvals carry across pages; a saved log updates the record and builds Monday's brief from approved lines only. Deals at risk and Imports are listed as later.
**My call:** Chose the full meeting loop over all four flows or a polished core only.

### Prompt 9 · More of the CRM
**As written:** "Some features to consider for AI integration: the CRM keeps lists of clients and lets users group them; it keeps a log of client interactions (in person, online, meetings, conferences, high stakes meetings); it keeps opportunities for each client, which shape the next interaction and who to prioritize for outreach." Then: "Go ahead, time is what we don't have."
**Refined prompt:**
> Add three core CRM jobs, each under the same rule (the AI drafts and explains, a person decides):
> 1. **Who to reach out to this week:** ranked, every reason sourced, reorder or snooze; AI spotted opportunities and stage changes wait for approval.
> 2. **Every kind of interaction:** calls, video, in person, events, email, tickets on one timeline, with logging from notes when there's no recording and a high stakes level that changes the rules.
> 3. **Lists in plain words:** show what was understood as chips I can remove, and why each account matches.

**Why:** the meeting loop covers one day; a CRM also has to answer who is next, what happened everywhere, and which accounts belong together.
**What came back:** three additions under the same rule (decision 0011): ranked outreach with sourced reasons and AI spotted opportunity changes behind the gate; an interaction timeline across types with coverage, logging from notes, and a high stakes level that changes the rules; lists typed in plain words, shown as removable chips with a reason for every match.
**My call:** Told you to build all three, in that order; Deals at risk folded into the ranked outreach.

### Prompt 10 · Ask, and a better dashboard
**As written:** "What industry did we choose for this exploration? The dashboard is still boring, think something innovative. The AI integration is ok-ish, where is my AI personal chat where I can ask a question about any client account and AI pulls out the answers and displays them in the top section above the summary board? Let's build this, take a couple of use cases, and animate this whole thing for the purpose of demo."
**Refined prompt:**
> Put an Ask panel at the top of Today. I type a question about any account and the answer appears above the board: show what it's reading, write the answer with a source chip on every claim, count up the key numbers, and end with a suggested next move. The board should react: the accounts the answer is about light up, the rest step back. Answers are read only. Add a Play demo mode that types and answers three questions in a row for screen recording, and respect reduced motion. Rebuild Today so it feels alive, not like a list.

**Why:** the prototype showed AI drafting; it didn't let a person simply ask.
**What came back:** the industry recap (B2B SaaS account teams; Larkspur sells retail analytics to retailers). Then Ask on Today: reading steps, an answer that writes itself with a source chip on every claim, counting numbers, a suggested next move, and a Play demo mode; three written use cases plus an answer for any account named. Today became a living board (day strip with a now line, account pulse tiles) that lights up the accounts an answer is about (decision 0012).
**My call:** Asked for the chat above the board and an animated demo; settled the industry recap (B2B SaaS account teams).

**Refined prompt:**
> Give every other tab one picture or interaction that answers its main question at a glance:
> - **Accounts:** a portfolio map.
> - **The account page:** who talks to whom.
> - **Opportunities:** a pipeline where AI suggestions wait as ghost cards.
> - **Review:** a triage mode with keyboard shortcuts.
